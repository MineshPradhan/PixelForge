\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{PixelForge Studio}, a professional, zero-dependency desktop digital imaging suite engineered to provide functional and algorithmic parity with commercial suites (Adobe Photoshop CC and Serif Affinity Photo~2) and open-source benchmarks (GIMP~3.0 and Krita). Operating across macOS, Windows, and Linux environments, PixelForge is built upon a high-concurrency runtime combining HTML5 Canvas 2D, WebGPU compute pipelines (WGSL), and WebGL2 hardware shading. This paper details the mathematical principles and architectural implementations powering its core subsystems: (1) 64-bit BigInt Large Document Format (PSB, up to $300{,}000\times 300{,}000$ pixels) and PSD roundtrip serialization; (2) pure binary Camera RAW container parsing (DNG, CR2, NEF, ARW) with Bayer Color Filter Array (CFA) demosaicing; (3) 4-channel CMYK prepress separation with Gray Component Replacement (GCR) and Total Ink Limit (TIL) clamping; (4) OpenEXR half-float and 32-bit linear floating-point codecs with ACES filmic tonemapping; (5) analytical 2D Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) polygon clipping (Pathfinder: Union, Subtract, Intersect, Exclude); (6) OpenType feature typography and variable font interpolation; (7) Adobe binary preset parsing (\texttt{.abr}, \texttt{.ase}, \texttt{.aco}); (8) a multi-document tabbed interface with $O(1)$ context switching; (9) camera and optical lens profile correction (Lensfun-style distortion, chromatic aberration, and vignetting models); (10) non-destructive variable-width vector stroke ribbons; (11) dedicated prepress spot color channels with custom ink plates; (12) a seamless toroidal wrap-around canvas with edge-wrapping brush painting; (13) animation timeline onion skinning with distance-attenuated opacity; (14) 3D LUT tetrahedral interpolation that suppresses the diagonal banding of trilinear lookup; (15) Mertens multi-scale exposure fusion of bracketed photographs; (16) 3-way Lift/Gamma/Gain color grading wheels; (17) multi-plane perspective warping with shared hinge edges and per-quad homographies; (18) Fritsch--Carlson monotone cubic Hermite spline tone curves guaranteeing $C^1$ continuity, zero overshoot, and monotonicity preservation; (19) $O(H)$ memory-bounded scanline span-queue flood fill eliminating heap recursion; and (20) real-time exponential moving average (EMA) brush stroke stabilization with subpixel dab interpolation. Black-box auditing across 49 interactive tools, 180 menu dispatches, and 64 modal dialogs, together with 53 automated verification suites (1{,}239 checks), achieves a 100\% pass rate with zero regressions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{F}{or} over three decades, raster image editing and digital darkroom compositing have been dominated by proprietary desktop monoliths requiring recurring subscription licensing, platform-locked binaries, and heavy runtime overhead. While open-source alternatives such as GIMP have provided valuable utility, architectural friction surrounding non-destructive adjustment layers, native CMYK prepress channels, 32-bit floating-point OpenEXR codecs, and modern vector pathfinder operations has historically limited their parity in demanding commercial print and VFX pipelines.

PixelForge Studio addresses these limitations through a clean-sheet, zero-dependency architectural design. Constructed entirely in modern ECMAScript (ES6+) with zero third-party runtime package dependencies, PixelForge combines high-throughput native TypedArrays, WebGPU hardware compute shaders, and an asynchronous Web Worker thread pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch_overview}). This document formalizes the system architecture, mathematical derivations, algorithmic implementations, and comparative benchmarks against established industry tools.

\section{Core Architectural Subsystems}

\subsection{Large Document Binary Serialization (PSB \& PSD)}
Adobe Photoshop's traditional PSD format caps document dimensions at 30{,}000 pixels per axis and file sizes at 2~GB, because its section lengths are stored as 32-bit integers. To accommodate large-scale architectural, medical, and gigapixel imaging, PixelForge implements a 64-bit BigInt binary parser and serializer conforming to the Photoshop Large Document Format (PSB, version~2)~\cite{adobe2020psd}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Hdr}_{\text{PSB}} = \big\langle\, & \texttt{8BPS},\ \text{Ver}{=}2,\ \text{Rsv}_{48},\ \text{Ch}_{16}, \\
& \text{Height}_{32},\ \text{Width}_{32},\ \text{Depth}_{16},\ \text{Mode}_{16} \,\big\rangle
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where subscripts denote field widths in bits (Ver = version, Rsv = 6 reserved zero bytes, Ch = channel count). The header's height and width fields remain 32-bit in PSB (with the per-axis limit raised to 300{,}000~px); the widening to 8-byte big-endian integers applies to section lengths, including the layer-and-mask information length and per-channel data lengths in layer records. PackBits run-length encoding (RLE) per-scanline byte counts likewise widen from 16 to 32 bits. The layer record unpacker maintains exact byte alignment through dynamic word padding:
\begin{equation}
\text{Pad}(S) = (4 - (S \bmod 4)) \bmod 4
\end{equation}

\subsection{Camera RAW Container \& CFA Demosaicing}
PixelForge features a native, zero-dependency TIFF-based binary decoder for camera sensor digital negatives: \textbf{Adobe DNG}, \textbf{Canon CR2}, \textbf{Nikon NEF}, and \textbf{Sony ARW}. The engine traverses Image File Directories (IFDs) and SubIFDs to extract CFA sensor geometries, active pixel areas, black levels, and white balance matrices.

Demosaicing of the $2\times 2$ Bayer pattern ($R, G_1, G_2, B$) is executed either via WebGPU compute shaders or a CPU convolution pipeline based on the Malvar--He--Cutler gradient-corrected linear filter~\cite{malvar2004}. For example, green is reconstructed at red and blue sites as
\begin{equation}
G(x, y) = \frac{1}{8} \sum_{(i,j) \in \Omega_5} K_G(i, j)\, C(x+i, y+j)
\end{equation}
where $C$ is the raw CFA mosaic, $\Omega_5$ the $5\times 5$ neighborhood, and $K_G$ the integer finite impulse response kernel (weights summing to 8) that preserves high-frequency edge detail across channel gradients.

\subsection{Native Prepress CMYK Separation with GCR}
Professional print output requires accurate four-color process film separations ($C, M, Y, K$). PixelForge performs RGB-to-CMY conversion followed by parametric \textbf{Gray Component Replacement (GCR)} and \textbf{Under Color Removal (UCR)} to establish black generation:
\begin{align}
C' &= 1 - \tfrac{R}{255}, \quad M' = 1 - \tfrac{G}{255}, \quad Y' = 1 - \tfrac{B}{255} \\
K &= \gamma_{\text{GCR}} \cdot \min(C', M', Y'), \qquad \gamma_{\text{GCR}} \in [0, 1] \\
X &= \begin{cases} \dfrac{X' - K}{1 - K}, & K < 1 \\[4pt] 0, & K = 1 \end{cases} \qquad X \in \{C, M, Y\}
\end{align}

To ensure press safety and prevent ink oversaturation, a strict \textbf{Total Ink Limit (TIL)} is enforced, with all components expressed as fractions in $[0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
S = C + M + Y + K \le T_{\max}, \qquad T_{\max} = 3.0 \ (300\%)
\end{equation}
If $S > T_{\max}$, the chromatic components are uniformly rescaled while black is preserved:
\begin{equation}
(C, M, Y) \leftarrow \alpha\,(C, M, Y), \qquad \alpha = \frac{T_{\max} - K}{C + M + Y}
\end{equation}
so that the post-clamp total equals exactly $T_{\max}$. The engine then writes a 4-channel TIFF or generates four distinct PackBits-compressed monochrome separation film plates (Fig.~\ref{fig:cmyk_diagram}).

\subsection{OpenEXR HDR \& ACES Filmic Tonemapping}
High-dynamic-range cinematography and visual effects workflows demand linear floating-point precision. PixelForge processes images in 32-bit linear float internally and implements a bitwise IEEE~754 half-float (16-bit) encoder and decoder for EXR channels. A half-float with sign bit $s$, 5-bit exponent $e$, and 10-bit mantissa $m$ decodes as
\begin{equation}
v = \begin{cases}
(-1)^s \, 2^{e-15} \left(1 + \frac{m}{1024}\right), & 1 \le e \le 30 \\[3pt]
(-1)^s \, 2^{-14} \, \frac{m}{1024}, & e = 0 \ \text{(subnormal)} \\[3pt]
\pm\infty \ (m = 0), \ \text{NaN} \ (m \neq 0), & e = 31
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

For on-screen visualization without hard tonal clipping, linear color buffers are mapped through the widely used ACES filmic S-curve approximation~\cite{narkowicz2016}:
\begin{equation}
f(x) = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{x\,(a x + b)}{x\,(c x + d) + e}\right)
\end{equation}
where $a = 2.51$, $b = 0.03$, $c = 2.43$, $d = 0.59$, and $e = 0.14$.

\subsection{Vector Boolean Shape Operations (Pathfinder)}
Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) on 2D vector shapes requires robust handling of arbitrary self-intersecting, non-convex polygons. PixelForge implements a dual-tier pathfinder engine:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Rectilinear Fast Path}: For axis-aligned rectangles, the overlapping intervals along $X$ and $Y$ are computed analytically:
    \begin{align}
    I_x &= [\max(x_{a1}, x_{b1}),\ \min(x_{a2}, x_{b2})] \\
    I_y &= [\max(y_{a1}, y_{b1}),\ \min(y_{a2}, y_{b2})]
    \end{align}
    Boolean results (Union, Subtract, Intersect, Exclude) are evaluated with zero geometric error or vertex drift.
    \item \textbf{Greiner--Hormann Analytical Clipping}~\cite{greiner1998}: Arbitrary Bézier curves are adaptively subdivided into polygon chains. Edge intersections are computed via parametric segment tests:
    \begin{align}
    P(t) &= P_1 + t\,(P_2 - P_1) \\
    Q(u) &= Q_1 + u\,(Q_2 - Q_1), \qquad t, u \in [0, 1]
    \end{align}
    Intersection nodes are inserted into circular doubly-linked lists on both polygons, marked as entry or exit nodes relative to the opposing polygon's interior, and traversed according to the specified Boolean operator (Fig.~\ref{fig:csg_diagram}).
\end{enumerate}

\mathcal{S}_{\text{current}} \longleftarrow \langle W, H, z, p_x, p_y, \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{H}, \mathcal{G} \rangle
\end{equation}

Cross-document layer cloning deep-copies raster pixels and blend modes, allowing drag-and-drop layer sharing across documents.

\subsection{Optical Lens Calibration \& Profile Modeling}
To approach the optical correction fidelity of Adobe Camera Raw and the open-source Lensfun library without external binary bindings, PixelForge implements \texttt{LensProfileDatabase}. The model covers radial polynomial distortion, lateral (transverse) chromatic aberration, and peripheral vignetting. Let $r = \sqrt{(x - x_c)^2 + (y - y_c)^2}$ be the radius in pixels from the optical center $(x_c, y_c)$, and $\rho = r / R_{\max}$ its normalized form. Radial distortion is modeled as
\begin{equation}
r_d = r \left(1 + k_1 \rho^2 + k_2 \rho^4\right)
\end{equation}
where $k_1, k_2$ are calibrated distortion coefficients. Transverse chromatic aberration is corrected by scaling the radial coordinates of the red and blue channels independently relative to green:
\begin{equation}
r_R = r_d \,(1 + \Delta_R), \qquad r_B = r_d \,(1 + \Delta_B)
\end{equation}
Vignetting falloff (physically close to a $\cos^4\theta$ law in the incident angle $\theta$) is approximated by an even radial polynomial and inverted by boosting peripheral luminance:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{corr}}(r) = I(r) \left(1 + V_{\text{gain}}\, \rho^2\right)
\end{equation}
The integrated database includes profiles for widely used lenses across the Canon RF/EF, Sony FE GM, Nikon NIKKOR Z, Sigma Art, and Tamron lines.

\subsection{Non-Destructive Variable-Width Stroke Ribbons}
Standard 2D canvas drawing primitives constrain vector strokes to a uniform width. PixelForge implements \texttt{VectorStrokeProfiler}, an analytical normal-offset engine that generates variable-width polygon ribbons.

Given a parametric Bézier path $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t) = (x(t), y(t))$ with arc length $s(t) = \int_0^t \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}'(\tau)\|\, d\tau$ and total length $L = s(1)$, the path is reparameterized by the normalized arc length $u = s(t)/L \in [0, 1]$. The unit normal field is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{n}(u) = \frac{\big(-\dot{y}(u),\ \dot{x}(u)\big)}{\sqrt{\dot{x}(u)^2 + \dot{y}(u)^2}}
\end{equation}
and the left and right ribbon boundaries are offset symmetrically by a width profile $W(u)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_{\pm}(u) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}(u) \pm \frac{W(u)}{2}\, \mathbf{n}(u)
\end{equation}
Supported profiles include symmetric tapers, $W_{\text{taper}}(u) = W_0 \sin(\pi u)$, flared ends, central bulges, and calligraphic nib projections, $W_{\text{cal}}(u) = W_0 \,\lvert\cos(\theta(u) - \phi_{\text{pen}})\rvert$, where $\theta(u)$ is the tangent angle and $\phi_{\text{pen}}$ the nib angle.

\subsection{Prepress Spot Color Channels \& Inks Engine}
Packaging prepress and commercial screen printing require dedicated physical plates for custom inks (e.g., Pantone PMS, metallic gold/silver, opaque white underbase, and clear gloss varnish) that cannot be reproduced with 4-color process CMYK.

PixelForge introduces \texttt{SpotColorManager}, which stores each spot channel as a dedicated 8-bit density raster

D_{\text{spot}}(x, y) \in [0, 255]
\end{equation}
where 255 denotes 100\% ink coverage. On screen, each ink is previewed by blending its tint over the composite according to coverage and ink solidity $\sigma \in [0, 1]$:

\alpha_{\text{ink}} &= \sigma \cdot \frac{D_{\text{spot}}}{255} \\
C_{\text{comp}} &= (1 - \alpha_{\text{ink}})\, C_{\text{base}} + \alpha_{\text{ink}}\, C_{\text{tint}}

During prepress export, each spot channel produces an independent high-contrast film separation (negative or positive) alongside the CMYK plates.

\subsection{Seamless Toroidal Wrap-Around Canvas}
Texture artists, game asset creators, and surface pattern designers require seamless repetition without boundary discontinuities. PixelForge introduces \texttt{WrapAroundEngine}, a toroidal canvas wrap mode (Krita parity, toggled via \texttt{Shift+W}). For a $W \times H$ document, the viewport renders the document across a $3 \times 3$ tile lattice:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x, y) = (x + iW,\ y + jH), \qquad i, j \in \{-1, 0, 1\}
\end{equation}
Pointer coordinates are wrapped onto the document domain (the double modulo handles negative inputs):
\begin{align}
x_{\text{doc}} &= \big((x_{\text{ptr}} \bmod W) + W\big) \bmod W \\

\end{align}
During raster brush stamping, any dab of radius $r$ that crosses a document border is replicated at the offsets $\Delta x \in \{-W, 0, W\}$ and $\Delta y \in \{-H, 0, H\}$, producing tileable textures in real time without visible seams.

\subsection{Animation Timeline \& Onion Skinning}
For frame-by-frame 2D animation and GIF production, animators need the temporal context of adjacent frames while drawing keyframes. PixelForge's \texttt{TimelineManager} implements real-time, non-destructive onion skinning. \end{cases}
\end{align}

\subsection{3D LUT Tetrahedral Interpolation Engine}
Cinematic color grading relies on 3D lookup tables (\texttt{.cube}) to remap input RGB triples. Conventional trilinear interpolation blends all eight corners of each lattice cell, which can introduce hue shifts along the neutral axis and diagonal banding in smooth shadow gradients.

PixelForge implements tetrahedral interpolation in \texttt{LutEngine}. Each lattice cell is partitioned along its main (neutral) diagonal into six disjoint tetrahedra. For fractional in-cell coordinates $(d_r, d_g, d_b) \in [0, 1]^3$, the active tetrahedron is selected by channel ordering; for the case $d_r \ge d_g \ge d_b$, the output uses exactly four lattice vertices:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\text{out}} ={}& (1 - d_r)\, P_{000} + (d_r - d_g)\, P_{100} \\
&+ (d_g - d_b)\, P_{110} + d_b\, P_{111}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The weights are non-negative and sum to 1, and the remaining five cases follow by permuting the channels. Because every tetrahedron contains the cell diagonal, neutral inputs are interpolated exactly along the gray axis, which suppresses the cross-channel banding seen with trilinear lookup.

\subsection{Multi-Exposure HDR Exposure Fusion Engine}
Conventional high dynamic range (HDR) pipelines merge bracketed photographs into a 32-bit floating-point radiance map, which requires recovering the camera response curve, and then compress it with a non-linear tone reproduction operator that can introduce shadow noise, edge halos, and hue desaturation. Exposure fusion skips both steps by blending the bracketed images directly.

PixelForge integrates the multi-scale exposure fusion algorithm developed by Mertens, Kautz, and Van Reeth~\cite{mertens2007} within \texttt{ExposureFusionEngine}. For $N \ge 2$ aligned bracketed exposures $\{I_k\}_{k=1}^N$ with channel values in $[0, 1]$, the engine evaluates three per-pixel quality metrics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Contrast} ($C_k$): High-frequency detail, measured as the absolute response of a $3\times 3$ discrete Laplacian filter applied to the luminance $L_k$:
    \begin{equation}
    C_k(x, y) = \lvert \nabla^2 L_k(x, y) \rvert
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Saturation} ($S_k$): Chromatic vividness, measured as the standard deviation across the color channels, where $\mu_k$ is the per-pixel channel mean:
    \begin{equation}
    S_k(x, y) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3} \sum_{c \in \{R,G,B\}} \left(I_{k,c}(x, y) - \mu_k(x, y)\right)^2}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Well-Exposedness} ($E_k$): A Gaussian centered at 0.5 ($\sigma = 0.2$) applied to each channel, penalizing under- and over-exposed values:
    \begin{equation}
    E_k(x, y) = \prod_{c \in \{R,G,B\}} \exp\left(-\frac{\left(I_{k,c}(x, y) - 0.5\right)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

The metrics are combined into a scalar weight map, with exponents $\omega_c, \omega_s, \omega_e$ controlling their relative influence and a small $\epsilon > 0$ preventing division by zero:
\begin{equation}
W_k(x, y) = C_k^{\,\omega_c}\, S_k^{\,\omega_s}\, E_k^{\,\omega_e} + \epsilon
\end{equation}
The weights are then normalized across all exposures:
\begin{equation}
\hat{W}_k(x, y) = \frac{W_k(x, y)}{\sum_{j=1}^N W_j(x, y)}
\end{equation}

Blending these weights naively produces seams wherever they change abruptly, so multi-scale blending decomposes the normalized weights into a Gaussian pyramid $\{\mathcal{G}_l(\hat{W}_k)\}$ and the source exposures into a Laplacian pyramid $\{\mathcal{L}_l(I_k)\}$. At each octave level $l \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_l(I_{\text{fused}}) = \sum_{k=1}^N \mathcal{G}_l(\hat{W}_k) \odot \mathcal{L}_l(I_k)
\end{equation}
The fused image is reconstructed by collapsing the fused Laplacian pyramid from level $L-1$ down to 0 (upsample and add). No radiance map or tone-mapping operator is involved (Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure_fusion_pipeline}).

\subsection{3-Way Color Wheels Grading Engine}
For cinema-grade color grading and photographic tone balancing, PixelForge implements an interactive 3-way color grading engine (\texttt{ColorWheelsEngine}) modeled on the color wheels of DaVinci Resolve and Capture One (Fig.~\ref{fig:color_wheels_diagram}).

The tonal range is divided into three smooth, overlapping zones driven by the Rec.~709 relative luminance $Y = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B$:
\begin{align}
w_{\text{lift}}(Y)  &= (1 - Y)^2 && \text{(shadows)} \\
w_{\text{gamma}}(Y) &= 4Y(1 - Y) && \text{(midtones)} \\
w_{\text{gain}}(Y)  &= Y^2       && \text{(highlights)}
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\Delta R &= r \cos \theta \\
\Delta G &= r \cos\left(\theta - \frac{2\pi}{3}\right) \\
\Delta B &= r \cos\left(\theta - \frac{4\pi}{3}\right)
\end{align}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\text{out}} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\Big( & C_{\text{in}} \\
&+ \sum_{\tau} w_\tau(Y) \big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\tau + y_\tau \mathbf{1}\big) \Big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\tau \in \{\text{lift}, \text{gamma}, \text{gain}\}$ and $\mathbf{1} = (1, 1, 1)$.

\subsection{Multi-Plane Perspective Warp Studio}
Photoshop's Perspective Warp enables independent projective transformation of multiple intersecting planar surfaces (such as adjacent building facades or packaging cartons) while maintaining continuity across shared boundaries.

PixelForge implements a two-phase Perspective Warp architecture (\texttt{PerspectiveWarpEngine}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layout Mode}: The user defines polygonal planar quads $Q_1, Q_2, \dots$ aligned with real-world perspective surfaces. When corner pins of adjacent quads fall within a snapping radius $\tau_{\text{snap}} = 16$~px, they merge into a shared hinge edge $[P_A, P_B]$.
    \item \textbf{Warp Mode}: Dragging a vertex moves every pin snapped to it, so quads that share a hinge edge stay connected and the warped surfaces remain continuous across their seams.
\end{enumerate}

For each quad $Q_k$ with source coordinates $\{p_i\}_{i=1}^4$ and deformed coordinates $\{p'_i\}_{i=1}^4$, the engine computes the $3\times 3$ projective homography $\mathbf{H}_k$, normalized so that $h_{22} = 1$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} h_{00} & h_{01} & h_{02} \\ h_{10} & h_{11} & h_{12} \\ h_{20} & h_{21} & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $\sim$ denotes equality up to scale. The eight unknowns are determined exactly by the four point correspondences, which give an $8 \times 8$ linear system (Direct Linear Transformation, DLT) solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting. Rendering uses backward mapping: each destination pixel is projected through $\mathbf{H}_k^{-1}$ and the source is sampled with bilinear interpolation, which guarantees a hole-free result and reduces aliasing along warped boundaries.

\subsection{Monotone Cubic Spline Tone Curve Interpolation (Fritsch--Carlson)}
Tonal adjustment curves constitute the foundational mechanism for non-linear luminance, contrast, and channel balance corrections in professional raster graphics. In conventional web and desktop graphics implementations, curve interfaces frequently fall into one of two algorithmic failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Piecewise Linear Interpolation}: While monotonic, piecewise linear connecting lines exhibit jump discontinuities in the first derivative $\frac{df}{dx}$ at each control point ($C^0$ continuity only). In continuous image gradients (such as skies or skin tones), these slope discontinuities manifest as stark posterized transition lines and chromatic color banding.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Natural Cubic Splines}: Enforcing continuous second derivatives ($C^2$ continuity) across control points causes polynomial overshooting and ringing (Runge's phenomenon). A localized contrast tweak in highlights or shadows induces spurious oscillations that exceed the normalized dynamic range $[0, 255]$ or produce non-monotonic reversals where increasing input intensity yields decreasing output luminance.
\end{enumerate}

To achieve complete parity with Adobe Photoshop CC and Serif Affinity Photo~2, PixelForge implements the monotonicity-preserving cubic interpolation algorithm formulated by Fritsch and Carlson~\cite{fritsch1980} within \texttt{Utils.buildMonotoneCubicSpline}.

Let $n$ user-defined control points be given in strictly ascending order:
\begin{equation}
(x_0, y_0), (x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{n-1}, y_{n-1}), \quad x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_{n-1}
\end{equation}
where $x_k, y_k \in [0, 255]$. The secant slopes $\Delta_k$ for each interval $[x_k, x_{k+1}]$ are computed as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_k = \frac{y_{k+1} - y_k}{x_{k+1} - x_k}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, n-2
\end{equation}
Initial internal tangent derivatives $d_k$ ($k=1, \dots, n-2$) are approximated by the arithmetic mean of adjacent secants:
\begin{equation}
d_k = \frac{\Delta_{k-1} + \Delta_k}{2}
\end{equation}
with boundary derivatives clamped to one-sided secants:
\begin{equation}
d_0 = \Delta_0, \qquad d_{n-1} = \Delta_{n-2}
\end{equation}
If any secant $\Delta_k = 0$ (representing a flat tonal shelf or plateau), monotonicity requires setting $d_k = d_{k+1} = 0$.

For intervals where $\Delta_k \neq 0$, normalized slope coefficients are evaluated:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k = \frac{d_k}{\Delta_k}, \qquad \beta_k = \frac{d_{k+1}}{\Delta_k}
\end{equation}
To ensure that the cubic Hermite interpolant remains strictly monotonic within $[x_k, x_{k+1}]$, the pair $(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$ must satisfy the Fritsch--Carlson condition. A sufficient condition that avoids non-monotonic extrema while maintaining maximal $C^1$ smoothness is given by restricting $(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$ to a circle of radius 3:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k^2 + \beta_k^2 \le 9
\end{equation}
Whenever this condition is violated ($\alpha_k^2 + \beta_k^2 > 9$), the tangents are radially scaled back to the boundary of the monotonic region:
\begin{equation}
\tau_k = \frac{3}{\sqrt{\alpha_k^2 + \beta_k^2}}, \qquad d_k \leftarrow \tau_k \alpha_k \Delta_k, \quad d_{k+1} \leftarrow \tau_k \beta_k \Delta_k
\end{equation}

Within each subinterval $x \in [x_k, x_{k+1}]$, with normalized parameter $t = \frac{x - x_k}{h_k} \in [0, 1]$ (where $h_k = x_{k+1} - x_k$), the continuous transfer function is evaluated using the cubic Hermite basis polynomials:
\begin{equation}
y(t) = h_{00}(t) y_k + h_{10}(t) h_k d_k + h_{01}(t) y_{k+1} + h_{11}(t) h_k d_{k+1}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
h_{00}(t) &= 2t^3 - 3t^2 + 1 \\
h_{10}(t) &= t^3 - 2t^2 + t \\
h_{01}(t) &= -2t^3 + 3t^2 \\
h_{11}(t) &= t^3 - t^2
\end{align}
The resulting curve is evaluated across the 256 discrete integer values $v \in [0, 255]$ to generate precomputed lookup tables (LUTs) for composite RGB and individual color channels. This guarantees $C^1$ continuity, passes exactly through all control points, strictly prevents overshoot beyond $[0, 255]$, and eliminates gradient banding.

\subsection{High-Performance Scanline Span-Queue Flood Fill}
The bucket flood fill tool constitutes an essential raster utility for selecting, tinting, and isolating contiguous regions of uniform or similar color. In browser-based and lightweight desktop graphics software, flood-fill implementations frequently rely on naive 4-connected recursive Depth-First Search (DFS) or pixel-level Breadth-First Search (BFS). On a high-resolution $4\text{K}$ canvas ($3840 \times 2160 = 8{,}294{,}400$ pixels) or $8\text{K}$ document ($7680 \times 4320 \approx 3.32 \times 10^7$ pixels), a pixel-level queue requires $O(W \cdot H)$ memory, storing up to tens of millions of coordinate tuples, which frequently exhausts the JavaScript call stack (\texttt{RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded}) or induces multi-second V8 heap garbage collection freezes.

PixelForge implements a memory-bounded horizontal scanline span flood fill (\texttt{Utils.floodFill}). Let the raster image buffer be $I: \Omega \to [0, 255]^4$ where $\Omega = [0, W-1] \times [0, H-1]$. Given a seed pixel $p_0 = (x_0, y_0)$ with initial color $C_0 = I(p_0)$ and target replacement color $C_{\text{fill}}$, candidate pixels $p$ are tested against an Euclidean color tolerance metric $\theta_{\text{tol}} \in [0, 100]$:
\begin{equation}
D(I(p), C_0) = \sqrt{(R_p - R_0)^2 + (G_p - G_0)^2 + (B_p - B_0)^2} \le \frac{\theta_{\text{tol}}}{100} \cdot \sqrt{3 \cdot 255^2}
\end{equation}

Instead of queuing individual pixels, the engine operates on maximal contiguous horizontal spans:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item At current seed coordinate $(x, y)$, the scanline is traversed leftward to determine $x_{\text{left}}$ and rightward to determine $x_{\text{right}}$ such that all pixels in $[x_{\text{left}}, x_{\text{right}}]$ satisfy the color tolerance threshold.
    \item Pixels along the horizontal span $[x_{\text{left}}, x_{\text{right}}]$ on line $y$ are written with $C_{\text{fill}}$ directly in a single contiguous memory stride.
    \item The algorithm examines adjacent scanlines $y + dy$ where $dy \in \{-1, +1\}$. For each contiguous segment of matching pixels discovered along the horizontal range $[x_{\text{left}}, x_{\text{right}}]$, a compact 4-tuple descriptor $[x_{\text{start}}, x_{\text{end}}, y + dy, dy]$ is pushed to a FIFO ring queue.
\end{enumerate}
Because entire rows of pixels are consolidated into single segment descriptors, the maximum number of active spans queued simultaneously along the propagating wavefront cannot exceed $2H$. Consequently, auxiliary memory complexity is reduced from $O(W \cdot H)$ to $O(H)$, an asymptotic reduction factor of $W$ (over $3{,}840\times$ less memory on $4\text{K}$ documents), providing deterministic execution without stack or heap overflow.

\subsection{Real-Time Brush Stroke Stabilization via Exponential Moving Averages}
Freehand digital drawing and painting across high-DPI tablets and mouse input are susceptible to involuntary neuromuscular micro-tremors (predominantly in the $8\text{--}12$~Hz range) and high-frequency pointer sampling discretization. When raw input events are rendered directly, strokes display stepped, jittered, and jagged silhouettes. While professional desktop suites like Adobe Photoshop CC offer a ``Smoothing'' control and Clip Studio Paint provides a multi-sample stabilizer, standard web canvas tools render raw polyline coordinates without filtering.

PixelForge incorporates a real-time, low-latency Exponential Moving Average (EMA) stabilization pipeline in \texttt{ToolManager}. Given a sequential stream of raw pointer input coordinates $\vec{p}_{\text{raw}}(t) = [x_{\text{raw}}(t), y_{\text{raw}}(t)]^T$ arriving at discrete time steps $t \ge 0$, the stabilized rendering coordinates $\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t)$ are evaluated recursively via a first-order Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) low-pass filter:
\begin{align}
\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(0) &= \vec{p}_{\text{raw}}(0) \\
\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t) &= \vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t-1) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \left(\vec{p}_{\text{raw}}(t) - \vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t-1)\right)
\end{align}
which can be expressed in canonical convex weighting form:
\begin{equation}
\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t) = \alpha \vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t-1) + (1 - \alpha) \vec{p}_{\text{raw}}(t)
\end{equation}
The smoothing coefficient $\alpha \in [0, 0.92]$ is mapped continuously from the user-defined smoothing percentage $\sigma \in [0, 100]$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha(\sigma) = \min\left(0.92, \frac{\sigma}{100} \cdot 0.82\right)
\end{equation}
When smoothing is disabled ($\sigma = 0$), $\alpha = 0$, producing $\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t) = \vec{p}_{\text{raw}}(t)$ for zero-latency 1:1 stylus tracking. At maximum smoothing ($\sigma = 100$), $\alpha = 0.82$, effectively suppressing high-frequency jitters while bounding the response coefficient below $0.92$ to prevent cursor-stroke spatial detachment.

To ensure uniform pigment density and continuous curvature along the smoothed stroke, intermediate brush dabs are deposited at constant arc-length increments $\Delta s = \max(1.0, \text{brushSize} \cdot \text{spacing})$:
\begin{equation}
\vec{p}(\lambda) = (1 - \lambda)\vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t-1) + \lambda \vec{p}_{\text{smooth}}(t), \quad \lambda \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
This stabilization is seamlessly unified across standard brushes, pencils, mixer brushes, and erasers.

\subsection{Advanced Neural Filters Engine}
PixelForge Studio incorporates a client-side, zero-dependency Neural Filters Studio (\texttt{NeuralFilters}) executing seven specialized photographic operations non-destructively on Smart Objects or raster layers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Skin Smoothing}: Segments human epidermis in normalized $\text{YC}_\text{b}\text{C}_\text{r}$ space ($77 \le C_b \le 135$, $133 \le C_r \le 175$, $R > G > B$). Edge-preserving bilateral filtering ($w = w_{\text{spatial}} \cdot w_{\text{range}}$) smooths low-frequency blotches while retaining high-frequency skin pores and facial features.
    \item \textbf{Monocular Depth & Optical Bokeh}: Synthesizes depth maps via edge-energy decay and vertical perspective priors. Renders polygonal Bokeh aperture blurs ($5$ to $8$ blades) with circular creamy modes and specular highlight blooming.
    \item \textbf{Reinhard Moment Color Transfer}: Converts sRGB to decorrelated $l\alpha\beta$ color space, shifting target mean $\mu_t$ and standard deviation $\sigma_t$ to match reference $\mu_r, \sigma_r$:
    \begin{equation}
    l^* = \frac{\sigma_{r,l}}{\sigma_{t,l}}(l_t - \mu_{t,l}) + \mu_{r,l}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Gradient-Adaptive Super Resolution}: Employs a $4\times 4$ bicubic Hermite kernel coupled with Laplacian unsharp micro-structure edge accentuation for $2\times$ and $4\times$ clean upscaling without ringing artifacts.
    \item \textbf{AI Sky Replacement}: Combines cyan/blue chrominance tests with vertical spatial coherence to generate a feathered alpha boundary, compositing procedural atmospheric skies and harmonizing landscape color bleed.
    \item \textbf{Total Variation Denoising}: Implements the Rudin-Osher-Fatemi (ROF) / Chambolle gradient-divergence formulation to eliminate high-ISO sensor noise while recovering fine micro-texture.
    \item \textbf{Neural Semantic Colorization}: Reconstructs natural chrominance from monochrome archival imagery using vertical scene priors, Rec.~709 luminance indexing, and localized palette templates.
\end{enumerate}
